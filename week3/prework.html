Part A — Load the French factor file

  1. Go to https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html
  2. Under U.S. Research Returns Data, find Fama/French 3 Factors, click CSV, unzip.
  3. Open the CSV in a text editor first. Find the four-line preamble, the header line ,Mkt-RF,SMB,HML,RF, the monthly block keyed 192607 onward, and the point where it stops and an annual block begins.
  4. Load only the monthly block in Python. Divide by 100. Print the last three rows.

What you should see, allowing for the file being updated since this was written:

         Mkt-RF     SMB     HML      RF
2026-05  0.0491 -0.0159 -0.0231  0.0031
2026-06 -0.0107  0.0339  0.0358  0.0029
2026-07 -0.0060 -0.0196  0.0211  0.0033

Write down, in one line, what Mkt-RF is and why you must not subtract RF from it.

Part B — Reproduce Apple

Pull Apple and SPY from yfinance with auto_adjust=True, resample to month-end, compute simple returns, and join to the French rows on year-month for 2015-07 to 2025-06. Assert 120 rows.

Then compute Apple's Sharpe ratio under the handout Part 2 standard and run the handout Part 3 regression. Targets:

Sharpe  0.788      alpha/yr 8.41%   t(α) 1.23    beta 1.134   R² 0.429

If your Sharpe is 0.79 and your beta is 1.13, your alignment is right and the rest of the week is the same code on more columns. If your beta is right and your Sharpe is not, look at the risk-free subtraction. If the row count is not 120, look at the join.

The full script is week3_worked.py; read it after you have tried, not before.

Part C — Read the papers

Both are on JSTOR through Purdue Libraries. Start here: https://www.lib.purdue.edu → Databases → JSTOR.

  • Sharpe (1964), Journal of Finance 19(3). Read the introduction and the section on equilibrium in the capital market, through the figure with the capital market line. About eight pages. Answer in one sentence: according to Sharpe, why does the market not reward idiosyncratic risk?
  • Jensen (1968), Journal of Finance 23(2). Read the introduction and the section that defines the regression. About six pages. Answer in one sentence: what would a fund manager's alpha be if the model were exactly right and the manager had no information?

Bring both sentences. They are the first thing Tuesday asks.

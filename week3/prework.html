Part A — Reproduce the comparison

Run the SPY/RSP block from handout.md Part 1 yourself.

Then answer, on paper, before class:

  1. Which of the two is "the S&P 500 return"?
  2. If a client asked you what the market did over this decade, which number would you give,

and what would you say alongside it?

There is no right answer to 1. There is a right behaviour for 2.

Part B — Find your benchmark's rules

Choose a candidate benchmark for your panel. Then find and write down its construction rules — not its price history.

Look for the index methodology document. Most major index providers publish one. Find:

  • What makes a security eligible for inclusion
  • How constituents are weighted
  • How often membership is reviewed, and what triggers a change outside the schedule
  • What happens to the index when a member is acquired or delisted

That last one should now interest you more than it would have two weeks ago.

If you cannot find a methodology document, that is a finding — write down where you looked. A benchmark whose rules you cannot state is a benchmark you cannot defend.

Part C — Reread your own catalog

Open data_quality_issues.md and read it as though someone else wrote it.

For each open issue, write one sentence: what claim would this stop me from making?

Be specific. Not "limits my analysis" — an actual sentence you would otherwise want to write, and now cannot.

Bring this list. Lab 3 Step 5 builds directly on it.

Part D — Write one paragraph

Two analysts compute the annualized volatility of the same security over the same window and get different answers. Neither has made an arithmetic error. Give three explanations, ranked by how likely you think each is.

Keep it. You will find out in the session whether your ranking matches the one professionals use — most people rank these in exactly the wrong order.

Bring to the session: the reproduced comparison with your two answers, your benchmark's construction rules, your list of forbidden claims, and this paragraph.

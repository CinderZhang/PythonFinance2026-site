Step 1 — State your conventions before you compute

Write down, before running anything, seven lines. The shape, not your answer, from the worked example:

Return type:     simple monthly returns from adjusted month-end close (yfinance, auto_adjust=True)
Frequency:       monthly; annualization ×12 for means and alphas, ×√12 for volatilities
Risk-free:       RF column, Ken French Data Library, Fama/French 3 Factors monthly
Window:          2015-07 to 2025-06, 120 months
Market series:   French Mkt-RF (regression); SPY (investable benchmark)
Equal-weight:    rebalanced monthly to 1/N
Std deviation:   sample (ddof = 1)

Every number you report for the rest of the course inherits these.

Step 2 — Build the excess-return panel

  1. Start here: https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html → U.S. Research Returns Data → Fama/French 3 Factors → CSV.
  2. Parse the monthly block only. Divide by 100. Key the rows on a monthly period.
  3. Resample your panel to month-end, compute simple returns, key on the same monthly period, and inner-join.
  4. Keep two frames: the raw monthly returns, which Step 3 needs for CAGR and wealth, and an excess frame with RF subtracted from every security column. Do not subtract it from Mkt-RF.
  5. Print the joined shape and assert the month count you declared in Step 1.

The shape, not your answer:

joined: (120, 14)    2015-07 -> 2025-06
first row 2015-07:  AAPL -0.0329   SPY 0.0226   Mkt-RF 0.0157   RF 0.0000
last row  2025-06:  AAPL  0.0215   SPY 0.0514   Mkt-RF 0.0486   RF 0.0034

If the row count is not what you declared, months were dropped in the join. Find them before going on.

Step 3 — Sharpe, stated properly

For every security and for the equal-weight panel, compute the Sharpe ratio under the Week 3 standard, and beside it the CAGR-over-volatility shortcut, in one table. The shape, not your answer:

             CAGR    ann vol   Sharpe (excess, arithmetic)   CAGR/vol, rf=0
AAPL       22.00%    27.81%              0.788                   0.791
EW panel   16.66%    13.38%              1.083                   1.245

Then two sentences: which names change rank between the two columns, and which column you will use for the rest of the course.

Step 4 — Jensen's regression

Regress each security's excess return, and the equal-weight panel's, on Mkt-RF. Report alpha per year, t(α), beta, t(β), R², and annualized residual volatility. The shape, not your answer:

             α/yr    t(α)     β     t(β)     R²    resid vol
AAPL        8.41%   1.23   1.134   9.41   0.429     21.1%
EW panel    5.89%   2.71   0.721  18.80   0.750      6.7%

Then answer, in writing: which alphas in your panel would you stand behind, by what threshold, and why the equal-weight panel's t-statistic is what it is relative to its members'.

Step 5 — Decompose the risk, and hand Week 4 its inputs

For every security and the equal-weight panel: total volatility, systematic volatility (|β| × market vol), idiosyncratic volatility (residual), and the systematic share. Check one name by hand: √(systematic² + idiosyncratic²) should come within about 1% of total; handout Part 4 explains the degrees-of-freedom gap. The shape, not your answer:

             β     total   systematic   idiosyncratic   share
AAPL      1.134   27.9%      18.2%         21.1%        43%
EW panel  0.721   13.4%      11.6%          6.7%        75%
check AAPL: sqrt(18.2² + 21.1²) = 27.9

Two sentences: your equal-weight panel's idiosyncratic volatility against the median single name, and what that says about how much diversification eight-to-fifteen names bought you.

Then save what Week 4 needs, in files you name and will load yourself next week:

  • your monthly excess returns, dated, one column per security, in decimals;
  • the annualized sample covariance matrix of those excess returns, in the same security order.

Week 4 does not care what you called them. It cares that the matrix loads back in the same order as the returns, so check that now.

Print the 3×3 corner of the covariance matrix and check that its diagonal is your Step 5 total volatility squared, using the unrounded volatility. The shape, not your answer: Apple 0.0776 = 0.2785².

Step 6 — Verify one beta against something you did not write

Pick one security. Start here: https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TICKER, statistics panel, Beta (5Y Monthly). Record the figure and the retrieval date.

Compare it with your Step 4 beta. If they differ, work the six checks in order: identity, frequency, window, return type, regressor, formula. Re-run yours on the matched window and conventions, using only complete months, and report both figures with their windows. Where the vendor does not state a convention, say which one you assumed and call the match provisional. The shape, not your answer: the Exxon entry in handout Part 7, which fails by 0.687, traces it to the window, and closes to 0.003 on sixty months ending 2026-07.

A gap that survives all six checks is a computation error, in one of you. Say which, or say unresolved.

Step 7 — Your benchmark, your defence

Compute alpha for your equal-weight panel three ways: on Mkt-RF, on the investable benchmark you chose in Step 1, and on the obvious alternative. The shape, not your answer: 5.89%, 5.23% on SPY, 8.04% on RSP.

Then defend. A colleague says your benchmark flatters your panel and you should have used the alternative. Write the strongest version of their case, including the number that supports it. Then answer it or concede it. Conceding a good objection earns full marks. Pretending the choice was not a choice earns nothing.

Step 8 — What your catalog allows you to claim

Reread data_quality_issues.md. Write a short section titled What these numbers can and cannot support. For each open issue, state what it does to an alpha, then choose one of the three responses: fix the input, bound the damage, or restrict the claim. Do it.

If a Blocker is still open, no headline alpha is reported this week. Say that plainly and report what you can.

Step 9 — What did you notice that I did not ask about?

The standing question.

Something in this week's computation behaved in a way the steps did not anticipate. A beta that halved between two windows. A name whose residual volatility dwarfs its peers. A covariance entry where the two matrices disagree by a lot. A Sharpe ratio that changed sign when the risk-free rate went in.

Write it down with evidence. Graded on genuineness and evidence, never on whether it was the right thing to notice. If you think one of this week's claims is overstated and can support that, this is where it goes.

If you want to do this differently

You may depart from the method above. A defended departure earns full marks. State what you did instead, why, and what you gave up.

Two departures are worked in the handout's script and are welcome here: add SMB and HML to the regression, the Fama-French three-factor model, and report how the alphas move; or run the same regression on daily data and compare the betas with the monthly ones. Either is a good Step 9 finding.

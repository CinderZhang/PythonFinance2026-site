Did it earn its risk?

A position returned 22% a year for ten years.

The portfolio manager asks one question.

Three numbers answer it. They come from one regression and one convention.

The paper

Sharpe (1964), Capital Asset Prices, Journal of Finance.

E[r_i] − r_f  =  β_i · ( E[r_m] − r_f )

Only market risk is rewarded. The rest can be diversified away, so the market does not pay for it.

Jensen (1968) turned it into a regression and named the intercept.

What it does not say: that a positive intercept is skill. It says the intercept should be zero, if the model is right.

Excess returns first

Every number this week is return minus the one-month bill rate, same month.

The bill rate is data: Ken French Data Library → Fama/French 3 Factors → RF.

202605,   4.91,  -1.59,  -2.31,   0.31
202606,  -1.07,   3.39,   3.58,   0.29
202607,  -0.60,  -1.96,   2.11,   0.33

Mkt-RF is already in excess of the bill rate. Subtract RF from it and you have subtracted it twice.

Mean bill rate over the window: 0.1571% a month. Zero in 2015. 0.34% in 2025.

The Sharpe ratio, stated properly

Sharpe = mean(r − r_f) / sd(r − r_f) × √12

Apple, 2015-07 to 2025-06, 120 months:

1.8291% / 8.0395% × √12  =  0.788

Three choices inside it: excess or raw · arithmetic or geometric · sample or population sd.

Geometric = CAGR: $1 → $7.30 in 120 months, so (7.3035)^(12/120) − 1 = 22.00%. Arithmetic ×12 = 23.83%. Sharpe is built on the arithmetic one.

Raw instead of excess: Apple 0.857. Coca-Cola 0.653 instead of 0.533.

The shortcut is not a Sharpe ratio

Sharpe (excess, arithmetic) CAGR ÷ vol, rf = 0
AAPL 0.788 0.791
MSFT 1.248 1.396
XOM 0.320 0.263
SPY 0.780 0.875

Same data. Different ranking. The right-hand column is what this page used to print.

State the three choices, every time.

One regression

r_i − r_f  =  α  +  β · ( r_m − r_f )  +  ε

Apple on French Mkt-RF:

α  =  0.0070 / month  →  8.41% / year     t = 1.23
β  =  1.134                                t = 9.41
R² =  0.429       residual sd  21.1% / year

95% interval on α: −0.43% to +1.83% a month. It includes zero.

Reading alpha

α / yr t(α) β t(β) R²
AAPL 8.41% 1.23 1.134 9.41 0.429
MSFT 15.22% 3.14 0.916 10.74 0.494
XOM −1.49% −0.19 0.862 6.33 0.254
PG 5.19% 1.05 0.348 3.99 0.119
EW panel 5.89% 2.71 0.721 18.80 0.750

Every t(β) near or above 4. One t(α) in eight above 2. Betas are precise; alphas are not. That never changes.

The equal-weight panel clears 2 when seven of its eight members do not. Nothing was added. Noise was averaged out.

Risk decomposition

total variance  =  β² · var(r_m − r_f)  +  var(ε)
                   systematic            idiosyncratic

Apple, annualized:

√( 18.2%²  +  21.1%² )  =  27.9%   ✓

R² is the systematic share. Procter & Gamble: 12%. Eighty-eight percent of its movement had nothing to do with the market.

Why diversification works

idiosyncratic vol systematic share
Single names, range 14% to 24% 12% to 55%
Equal-weight panel of eight 6.7% 75%

The systematic part did not shrink: 0.721 × 16.1%.

The idiosyncratic part collapsed. Eight unrelated residuals average toward zero.

That is Sharpe's argument in a table.

The covariance Week 4 needs

The annualized sample covariance of your excess returns. Eight names: 36 numbers, every one estimated from the same 120 months.

            AAPL    MSFT     JNJ
AAPL      0.0776  0.0346  0.0148
MSFT      0.0346  0.0439  0.0103
JNJ       0.0148  0.0103  0.0260

Diagonal = your volatilities squared. Off-diagonal = what the optimizer acts on. Apple–Microsoft correlation 0.59.

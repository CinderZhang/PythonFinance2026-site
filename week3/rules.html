Week 3 — Rules and Conventions

Register: RULES. No teaching here — see handout.md.

Conventions are declared, not assumed

Every reported metric carries its conventions: return type, annualization basis, risk-free assumption, and window. A number without them is not a result.

Once declared in Lab 3 Step 1, these bind for the rest of the course. If you change one later, say so and say why.

The verification standard

A metric is verified when it has been checked against a source you did not compute.

Acceptable outcomes, all of them:

  • Matches within a stated tolerance — verified
  • Differs, and the difference is explained by a convention or window — verified, explanation

recorded

  • Differs, was investigated, remains unexplained — reported as unresolved

Not acceptable: claiming verification without naming a source, or quietly adjusting your number to match theirs. If you change a number after seeing someone else's, say that is what happened and why it was right to.

The catalog constrains the claim

Open issues in data_quality_issues.md limit what you may report. A Blocker means no headline result.

Three honest responses per issue: fix the input, bound the damage, or restrict the claim. Choose one and record which.

Benchmarks are stated, never implied

Every comparison names its benchmark and the rules defining its membership. "Against the market" is not a benchmark.

Where a point-in-time membership file is unavailable, say so and state what it prevents you from claiming.

AI use

AI will compute any metric you ask for and will silently choose conventions for you. It annualizes on 252 or 365 depending on nothing in particular and does not mention which.

Check the conventions in generated code before you trust the output. This is the single highest-value review you can perform on AI-written analytics code this week.

What must be yours: the convention choices, the benchmark choice, the verification judgment, and the decision about what your catalog allows you to claim.

You must be able to explain every line of code you submit.

Reproducibility and citation

Unchanged: fresh-kernel runs, every number traceable to code, every source in source_log.md with access route, coverage window, and retrieval date.

Independent verification sources go in the source log too — including the ones that failed to resolve a gap.

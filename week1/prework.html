Part B — Generation 1: start the access requests

These are slow. Start them now.

WRDS — the research platform hosting CRSP, Compustat, IBES and TRACE. https://guides.lib.purdue.edu/wrds Register with your Purdue email. Purdue WRDS accounts are for faculty and graduate students, which includes you. The request goes to a local administrator for verification, so expect a queue. Install the Duo Mobile app first — WRDS login requires it, and that is the vendor's requirement, not Purdue's.

LSEG Workspace — available to all Purdue users. Register with your @purdue.edu address. Registration is handled by LSEG, not Purdue, and the welcome mail sometimes lands in spam.

Everything Purdue holds is on the A–Z list — https://guides.lib.purdue.edu/az/databases. Off-campus access uses your Purdue Career Account.

Where help comes from, and it is not us. Account and access problems go to the library or the vendor, both of whom can see your entitlements: parrlib@purdue.edu for WRDS and the business databases, LSEG's 24/7 live chat inside Workspace for anything Workspace-related, and https://lib.purdue.edu/contact for everything else. Tell us only if a request has gone quiet long enough to worry you — not so we can chase it, but so nothing in the course ends up waiting on it.

Optional, worth attempting: S&P Capital IQ and PitchBook. Both are lookup tools rather than pipelines — their licences restrict bulk export, so no lab depends on them.

Not in this course: Bloomberg. It is terminal-bound and cannot be scripted for coursework. Knowing why is the capability; nothing here needs it.

For each attempt, record: link used, date and time, resulting status, remaining blocker, and one sentence on what the platform is for. The graded Foundations record needs only WRDS and LSEG Workspace with date/status/barrier; the rest of this note-taking is class preparation.

A denial or a pending queue costs you nothing. The documented attempt is what is graded.

If you are blocked: contact Purdue business data library support, then the Libraries help channels, then the instructor — during prework, not the night before the lab.

Part D — Check a source fact against its documentation

Search for how to pull a price history from Refinitiv in Python. Take the first two or three results you would plausibly have copied.

Now check them against LSEG's current documentation. Thomson Reuters became Refinitiv; Refinitiv was acquired by LSEG; the Python package was renamed. How much of what you found is still correct?

Bring what you found and how long the check took. That interval — how long it takes to confirm a source fact rather than assume it — is the habit this course runs on.

Part E — Write one paragraph

You have two sources for the same security and they disagree about one number. What would make you decide the disagreement is a defect in the data rather than a difference in convention? Give one concrete test you could run.

Keep it. You will compare it against what you learn in the session, and the comparison is part of Lab 1.

Bring to Tuesday: a running notebook, your Generation 1 attempts, a Generation 2 key with something pulled, what your Refinitiv search turned up, and this paragraph.

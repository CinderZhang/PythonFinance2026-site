Build it, and it works

w \propto \Sigma^{-1}\mu
mu = returns.mean().values * 252
S  = returns.cov().values * 252
w  = np.linalg.solve(S, mu)
w  = w / w.sum()

Correct mathematics. Correct implementation.

Less return. Four times the volatility.

Beaten by a factor of 5.4.

Why: the matrix you inverted

8 assets → 36 distinct covariance entries, every one estimated with error.

Expected returns: worse.

Inverting a noisy matrix amplifies the noise — it does not average it out.

The optimizer finds where assets look most reliably different. That is exactly where the estimate is least trustworthy. Then it bets there.

The optimizer is an error-maximiser.

1/N wins because it estimated nothing.

What better data does — and does not — fix

Helps. Longer, cleaner history. More observations per parameter. CRSP includes the delisted names, so your covariance is not conditioned on survival.

Your Week 2 problem propagates straight into Σ.

Does not save you. The covariance structure itself changes.

2015–2020 and 2020–2025 are not two samples from one distribution. They are two regimes.

Better data makes your estimate honest. It does not make it certain.

Your constraints, your defence

"Your constraints are arbitrary. You picked that cap. A different one gives a different portfolio — so this is your choice, not the data's."

They are right that it is your choice.

That is not the same as being wrong.

Owning a stated choice is stronger than pretending the data decided.

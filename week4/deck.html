The question

Why does the optimizer hand you a portfolio nobody would hold?

And what does a desk actually do about it?

The paper

DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal (2009), Optimal Versus Naive Diversification, Review of Financial Studies.

Fourteen optimal strategies. Seven datasets. None beats 1/N consistently out of sample.

In their simulations, a sample-based optimizer on 25 assets needs about 3,000 months of history to be expected to beat equal weight.

What it does not say: that optimization is useless. It says the inputs are the problem, and every fix uses less of them.

The theory, in four lines

w  ∝  Σ⁻¹ μ         μ = mean monthly EXCESS return × 12

mu = ex.mean().values * 12
S  = ex.cov(ddof=1).values * 12
w  = np.linalg.solve(S, mu)
w  = w / w.sum()          # print the sum first; stop if it is not positive

Correct code. Higher Sharpe than anything else you can build from the same assets.

What you asked the data for

8 names → 36 covariances + 8 means = 44 parameters

60 months per window

1.36 months per parameter.

Not a degrees-of-freedom count: every month informs every estimate. Still the number to be uneasy about: sixty rows asked to pin down forty-four quantities.

Out of sample

Estimate on 2015–2020. Hold the weights, reset monthly, through 2020–2025. Compare with an eighth in each name.

window B excess return vol Sharpe gross
tangency from A 13.3% 30.5% 0.434 2.62
1/N 15.8% 13.6% 1.163 1.00

In sample the optimizer won by 2.2×. Out of sample it lost by 2.7×.

The gap is estimation error. That is the whole week.

Raise your hand: which side did you predict?

Why: the error maximizer

You did not measure Σ. You estimated 36 numbers from 60 months.

Inverting a noisy matrix amplifies the noise, most where two assets look almost interchangeable.

The optimizer bets hardest exactly where the estimate is least trustworthy.

1/N wins because it does not estimate anything.

What a desk does: long-only, with a cap

cap held in-sample Sharpe out-of-sample Sharpe
unconstrained 8, shorts 2.234 0.434
long-only 3 1.828 0.948
20% 7 1.420 0.948
15% 7 1.287 1.007
1/N 8 0.993 1.163

Long-only alone more than doubles the out-of-sample Sharpe. The cap makes it stable: largest swing 20 pp, not 81. Gross leverage 1.00 throughout.

One floor: N × cap ≥ 1, or no portfolio exists.

And 1/N still wins. That is the honest result. The remedy buys a portfolio you can defend, not one that beats naive.

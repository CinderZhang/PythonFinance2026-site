Part A — Load and sanity-check your estimates

Load the return series and covariance estimate you saved in Week 3.

Check three things:

  1. Does the covariance matrix have the shape you expect — one row and column per asset?
  2. Are the diagonal entries consistent with the individual volatilities you computed last

week? They should be, since the diagonal is variance. If they are not, you have a convention mismatch and this is a good time to find it.

  1. Is the matrix symmetric?

Any surprise here is a Week 3 problem surfacing late. Fix it now, not during the lab.

Part B — Count your parameters

Arithmetic, and it takes five minutes.

  • How many assets are in your panel? Call it $N$.
  • How many distinct entries does your covariance matrix have? $N(N+1)/2$.
  • How many expected returns are you estimating? $N$.
  • Total parameters: those two added together.
  • How many observations do you have — trading days in your window?
  • Observations per parameter: divide.

Write the last number down and bring it. It will be relevant.

Part C — Decide your constraints before you see any weights

This matters and the order matters.

Write down, before you optimize anything, what constraints you believe a portfolio built from your panel should carry. Long-only? A cap on any single position? A limit on gross leverage? None at all?

For each, one sentence on why.

The reason to do this now is that it is very easy to choose constraints after seeing an ugly result, and then present them as principled. Deciding first makes your defence in Lab 4 Step 6 honest, and you will be asked whether you decided before or after.

Part D — Write one prediction

You estimate a maximum-Sharpe portfolio on five years of data and hold it, unchanged, for the next five years. You compare it to putting an equal amount in each asset. Which does better out of sample, and by how much? Commit to an answer.

Write it down. Do not hedge — a specific wrong prediction is worth far more to you here than a vague safe one.

You will be asked to raise your hand for one side in the session.

Bring to the session: your loaded estimates, your observations-per-parameter number, your constraints with reasons, and your prediction.

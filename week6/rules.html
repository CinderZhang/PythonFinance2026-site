MSF Financial Modeling

Week 6 — Rules and Conventions

Register: RULES. No teaching here — see handout.md.

Every input is dated and labelled

Each figure in the model carries its source, the accession number or statement it came from, the date, and one of four labels: history, guidance, judgment, fact. An input you cannot date is recorded in the ledger as undated, with a note saying what you would need to date it. Undated inputs are permitted. Silently omitted ones are not.

Vintage is stated

This week's model is a current-state analysis: each year's figure as first reported in that year's own 10-K, with a check of whether a later 10-K changed it. Using one vintage for some inputs and another for others is a defect unless deliberate and disclosed.

Basis is stated

GAAP or non-GAAP. Fiscal or calendar. A one-time charge is named as one-time and excluded or included by a stated decision, never by silence.

Period comparability

Before any growth rate, confirm the periods are the same length. A comparison spanning a 53-week year is adjusted or flagged.

No balance, no valuation

Every projected year prints assets minus liabilities minus equity, and it is zero. Cash on the balance sheet equals opening cash plus the cash-flow statement's net change. Cash stays at or above the floor you set, or the revolver line shows the draw. The check block sits under every projection, and the valuation cell refuses to run without it.

Cash is computed last

From the cash-flow statement. Never a plug.

The terminal guard

The model raises an error when terminal growth is at or above the discount rate. The share of value coming from the terminal value is stated.

The known answer is the last filed year

Before any projection, the pipeline reproduces the company's own most recent filed year and prints the comparison against the filing: revenue, operating income, net income, the share count and net debt. A model that cannot reproduce what already happened does not get to project what has not.

The comparison is built the same way twice

Two companies are ranked only if both models share a horizon, a terminal convention, and a discount rate assembled from the same components, with only the beta and the leverage differing. Any other difference and the ranking measures the method, not the businesses.

A recommendation carries a flip point

The ranking names the single assumption that reverses it and the value at which it reverses, found by rerunning the model. Whether that value sits inside or outside the company's own history is stated.

A trigger has three parts

An observable that is published, a threshold with a direction, and a review date. Anything missing one of the three is not a trigger.

Edition A is written without AI, in the room

One page, no agent open, timestamped by the checkpoint that collects it: the price, the last filed revenue and net income, and the share count for each company, read by hand from WRDS or another professional source and written with source and date; then a paragraph on each company and a prediction of the ranking, the widths, and the assumption that will matter. It is never rewritten afterwards. The memo compares it with what the model produced and says what changed. The same numbers are later pulled in code and printed beside the by-hand read.

The share count is the latest filed count

From the most recent 10-Q or 10-K cover, dated, labelled as a fact. Not the year's weighted average, not a data feed's field.

Rerun, never add

Scenarios and cases are rerun through the whole model. Sensitivities are never summed.

Ranges, not points

The valuation is reported as a range against a dated market price, with the assumption most likely to move it named.

The reproducibility test

The ledger and the notebook must let someone who has never seen your work rebuild the value. This is the capstone's standard, applied one company early.

Your company is not the training case

At least one driver in your model exists because of your company and not because ABG had it, or one of ABG's is absent because your company does not work that way. Name it. Nineteen rows carried over unchanged under a new ticker is a renamed model, not a built one.

AI use

AI builds valuation models quickly and mostly correctly. It will also invent plausible-looking inputs, will not know the vintage of anything, will confidently produce a point estimate to two decimal places, and will read a standardised field as if it were the filing.

Never accept a number from an AI into your ledger without a source for it. What must be yours: the assumptions and their labels, the vintage choices, the sensitivity translated into sentences, the range, and the answer to the AI-risk check. You must be able to explain every line of code and every input you submit.

Reproducibility and citation

Unchanged: fresh-kernel runs, every number traceable, every source in source_log.md with access route, coverage window, and retrieval date.

Part A — Video 3: build the base case

Pro-Forma for ABG: build the base case — https://youtu.be/O4PeC2PqwRY (27 minutes; on YouTube, "Pro-Forma Valuation with AI Tutorial Part 1"). All four videos sit in one playlist, in course order: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLSzB59MC2JMQ.

Seven lines from three 10-Ks, nineteen assumptions with three labels, a five-year pro-forma built line by line with cash computed last, the check block, and the base case.

Watch for two things. The moment the standardised data-provider field for capital spending disagrees with the 10-K, and the rule that follows: no balance, no valuation.

Part B — Video 4: take it apart

Pro-Forma for ABG: take it apart and present — https://youtu.be/zDBUpjaQzpo (22 minutes; on YouTube, "Part 2").

The beta from sixty monthly returns and the seven discount rates it justifies, the terminal conventions, a tornado of nine shocks, three cases rerun rather than added, and the price the market implies.

Watch for one thing: the recession case is $210 and the four single shocks summed give $207. Why the model is rerun and never added is Thursday's work.

Part C — Pull both companies, before Tuesday

This is the part that decides whether Tuesday works. Pulling and aligning statements takes longer than modelling them, and Tuesday has no time for it.

For each of your two companies, open your AI partner in the folder you work in and send this, with the CIK filled in:

Pull company facts from https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK{ten-digit CIK}.json with a User-Agent header that names me, and cache the JSON locally. Using 10-K observations only, and each fiscal year's figure as first reported in that year's own 10-K, print a table in $ millions for the last three fiscal years: revenue, gross profit or cost of revenue, operating expenses, operating income, pretax income, income tax, net income, depreciation and amortization, operating cash flow, capital expenditure, inventory, PP&E net, total assets, shareholders' equity. Add the accession number and filed date for each year. Then tell me which of those lines is missing from the feed, and print each fiscal year's start date, end date and length in days.

Then, for each company, read four things off the filing itself and type them into the notebook with a comment naming the statement they came from, because the feed will not have all of them: capital expenditure, cash, total debt (current plus long-term), and the share count from the latest 10-Q or 10-K cover.

Start here for the filings: https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK={your CIK}&type=10-K

Two checks before you stop. Total assets less total liabilities equals equity, in the filed year. And every fiscal year in your window is the same length; if one is 53 weeks, write that on the row.

If a company will not yield these lines, say so now, by email, before Tuesday. Changing a pick on Tuesday costs you the session. Changing it tonight costs you an hour.

Part D — Rebuild ABG, and report where you stopped

Follow Video 3 and build the ABG model yourself. The known answer is $291.75 a share with 79.8% of the value in the terminal value.

You are not expected to finish, and Tuesday does not assume you did. The first thirty-five minutes of Tuesday build this model together, at the front, with everyone typing. Part D exists so that you arrive knowing where it gets hard for you, which makes those thirty-five minutes worth four times as much.

Find your own divergence. The handout's Part 4 to Part 6 print ABG's numbers year by year, and week6_worked.py beside them prints the whole projection. When your number differs, do not stare at the share price. Walk back: does your FY2026 revenue match 18,323.0? Your EBIT, 844.2? Your free cash flow to equity, 211.4? The first line that differs is the line to fix, and naming it is the skill.

Then report it, in the one-question Brightspace poll before Tuesday: finished and reproduced the answer, finished but a different number, stopped partway, or did not start. It is ungraded and it is not a test. It tells the instructor what Tuesday's first thirty-five minutes need to cover, and an honest "did not start" is more useful than a guess.

Bring to Tuesday: both companies' filed lines printed and sourced, your ABG rebuild however far it got, and the name of the first line where it diverged, if it did.

Part A — Confirm you can reach the filings

Find your company's CIK and confirm you can pull its filing history from EDGAR.

If your company does not file with the SEC — a foreign issuer, a private company — you have a problem worth discovering now rather than during the lab. Either switch companies or find the equivalent regulator's filings and confirm you can reach them.

A company you cannot source is a company you cannot value to this course's standard.

Part B — Run the revision query

Run the XBRL company-concept query on your company for at least two line items. Operating cash flow and revenue are good starting points.

Look for any period that appears with more than one value across filings.

Report what you find, including nothing. A negative result is a real result as long as you actually looked — say which concepts you checked and how many filings were covered.

If you do find a revision, note the size in dollars and as a percentage. Bring it; there will be time to compare across the room.

Part C — Establish the fiscal calendar

For your company, work out:

  • When does the fiscal year end? Is it a calendar year?
  • Are the recent annual periods all the same length?
  • Is there a 53-week year in your window?
  • Has the fiscal year end ever changed?

The last one is rarer and nastier than a 53-week year. A company that changes its year end produces a stub period, and any growth series spanning it is meaningless unless adjusted.

Part D — Write one paragraph

Before the session, and before you build anything:

Name the single assumption that will drive most of your company's valuation. Estimate, in your head, how much the valuation changes if that assumption moves by a modest amount. Then: what evidence would actually help you narrow it?

Most students discover their answer to the first question is "the terminal growth rate" and their answer to the third is "I do not know." Both are honest, and both are the right starting point.

Bring to the session: confirmed access to your filings, your revision query result, your fiscal calendar findings, and this paragraph.

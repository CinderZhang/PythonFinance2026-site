The build order — this is the part agents get wrong

1  income statement      revenue → gross profit → costs → D&A → EBIT
                         → interest on OPENING debt → tax → net income
2  balance sheet         inventory, PP&E, debt after paydown,
   from the drivers      equity after net income and buyback
3  FCFE                  NI + D&A + non-cash − capex − ΔWC + Δfinancing − paydown
4  CASH, LAST            opening cash + FCFE − buyback
                         revolver drawn ONLY if cash < floor
5  check block           balance identity · cash tie · cash floor
6  valuation cell        refuses if checks fail, or if g ≥ r

Cash is never a plug. If you can type a number into cash, the model is wrong.

Thursday: one exhibit, both companies

Ranges in dollars do not compare across two companies. Gaps do.

For each company: the gap between your range and its dated price, as a percentage.

Fair or nothing. Same horizon, same terminal convention, discount rates from the same components with only beta and leverage differing. Anything else and you are ranking your method.

The flip point

A recommendation with no flip point is an opinion.

The shape, not your answer:

We recommend A over B. The ranking turns on A's gross margin, held at 17.05%. At 15.9% the cases meet and B ranks higher. A's margin has not been below 16.4% in ten years, which is why the recommendation holds.

Find it by rerunning, not by arithmetic on the sensitivity table.

Then say whether that value sits inside or outside the company's own history. That sentence is the whole skill.

A trigger has three parts

Part Means Example
Observable published, you could look it up same-store gross margin, next 10-Q
Threshold a number, with a direction below 16.4%
Review date when you look Q1 filing, late April

"Monitor margins" is not a trigger.

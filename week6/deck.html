The year that had fifty-three weeks

2021-09-26 -> 2022-09-24    364 days = 52.00 weeks
2022-09-25 -> 2023-09-30    371 days = 53.00 weeks   <-- extra week
2023-10-01 -> 2024-09-28    364 days = 52.00 weeks

FY2023 had ~1.9% more selling days.

So FY2023 growth is inflated by a week — and FY2024 growth is depressed by the same week going away.

Two artefacts, opposite signs. Both look exactly like business trends.

The source ledger

Input: FY2017 operating cash flow Value: $63.598bn Source: Apple 10-K FY2017, filed 2017-11-03, via SEC EDGAR XBRL As-of: as first reported, 2017-11-03 Basis: GAAP, fiscal year (52/53-week, ends last Saturday of September) Note: Revised to $64.225bn in the FY2018 10-K. I use as-first-reported because this analysis reconstructs what was knowable at the time. A current-state analysis should use $64.225bn.

The note is the difference between an analyst and a spreadsheet.

Any link you cannot date is a link you cannot defend.

The model is the easy part

AI builds a DCF in seconds. That is not where the value is.

Where valuations actually go wrong:

  • Terminal value — usually most of the answer
  • Discount rate components, each with estimation error
  • Growth extrapolated from a 53-week year
  • Comparables chosen to fit a conclusion
  • Inputs of unknown vintage

Only one of those is arithmetic.

Report a range. A point estimate claims precision you do not have.

Your company, your judgment

"Your valuation is really just your terminal growth assumption wearing a spreadsheet."

They are probably right about the arithmetic.

The difference is not the number. It is whether the assumption is stated, sourced, and sensitivity-tested — or buried.

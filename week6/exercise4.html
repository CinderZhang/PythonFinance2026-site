Exercise 4 — Valuation Base Model: Company A

Register: DO. This page is the whole specification. The teaching is in the Week 6 handout and Videos 3 and 4; Lab 6 starts this model in the room and this exercise is its finished state.

This is the first of your two capstone companies, finished properly. Call it company A; which of your locked pair you pick is yours to choose, and the capstone then does the same for B and adds the comparison. Build this one well and the second is cheap, which is the point of building a pipeline rather than a spreadsheet.

The taught example is Asbury Automotive Group, in Videos 3 and 4, with its own published answer of $291.75 a share. Your company has no published answer, so it gets a better one — see requirement 7.

The data: you pull it

Your company's filed statements, obtained by you. Every number carries its source, reporting period, currency, units, and the date you extracted it.

  • SEC company-facts API (the Week 6 handout's route). Start here: https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK{your ten-digit CIK}.json, with a User-Agent header naming you.
  • The filings themselves, for everything the feed does not carry, which will include capital expenditure and the current share count. Start here: https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&type=10-K
  • Compustat on WRDS (professional lane): the fundamentals annual file. Record the variable names; Compustat's definitions are not the filing's line items, and the handout's Part 3 is about exactly that.

Establish the fiscal calendar before you compute any growth rate. If your company has a 53-week year or changed its year end, say so on the row.

Two sources, minimum, and where you have professional access, one of them is the professional source. Pending access is neither an excuse nor a penalty: document the attempt and use the filing. The attempt counts; the outcome never does.

The model: the capstone's per-company requirements, on one company

These are the same things the capstone requires for each company. Nothing here is extra.

  1. Grounding. Every input traces to a filed statement or a stated assumption, with source, period, currency, units and extraction date.
  2. Pro-forma statements and a five-year DCF. An explicit revenue driver, margin, tax rate, reinvestment and capital needs, free cash flow. Each driver labelled history, guidance or judgment. The statements balance and the model proves it: a check block under every projected year printing the balance identity, the cash tie between the balance sheet and the cash-flow statement, and cash against the floor you set. Cash is computed last and is never a plug. The valuation cell refuses to run when a check fails.
  3. Discount rate and terminal value, stated separately. Each component of the discount rate sourced or explicitly assumed, with the beta computed from returns rather than typed. Terminal growth below the discount rate, checked in code so the model refuses to run if it is not. State what fraction of the value comes from the terminal value; if it is most of it, which it usually is, say so plainly.
  4. The bridge. From what you discounted to value per share, with dated net debt and the latest filed share count. If you discounted cash flow to equity, as the videos do, equity value is direct and enterprise value is equity plus net debt, shown for the multiple check; if you discounted cash flow to the firm, subtract net debt.
  5. The assumptions ledger, every load-bearing row: value, unit, label, source and its date, one-sentence rationale, confidence. Including the rows you were unable to source, marked as such.
  6. Scenarios and sensitivity. Low, base and high cases, each rerun through the whole model, never added. A tornado over at least four drivers, ordered by swing. For the biggest bar, two sentences: what would have to be true about the business for the low end to be right, and for the high end.
  7. Checks, four kinds, at least one each.
  8. Known answer — your company's own. Before projecting anything, run your pipeline on the last fiscal year that already happened and print the comparison against the filing: revenue, operating income, net income, and the share count and net debt against the balance sheet. A model that cannot reproduce what already happened does not get to project what has not. This is read first.
  9. Reasonableness. An implied multiple against the company's own history or a peer.
  10. Edge case. The model fails loudly on an impossible input, such as terminal growth at the discount rate. Show it failing.
  11. AI risk. One thing an agent produced that you verified against a source, and what you changed if it was wrong.
  12. The honest range, against a market price dated within a week of submission, and the single assumption most likely to move it.
  13. One driver that is yours. Name a driver that exists in your company and not in ABG's, or one of ABG's that does not apply to yours, and say what you did about it. ABG's nineteen rows carried over unchanged under a different ticker is a renamed model, not a built one.

How it is read

The lab page and this page are the rubric.

The code is not graded; every agent can write a discounted cash flow. What is graded is what the code cannot decide: whether the last filed year reproduces, whether every number has a dated source and the right label, whether the checks are real and one of them caught something, whether the sensitivity is read as statements about the world, whether the range is honest against a price you dated, and whether the model is your company's rather than ABG's with a new ticker.

A defended departure earns full marks: a different driver, a different horizon, a firm-level discounted cash flow, multiples beside it, stated and justified.

A notebook without visible executed output is read as unfinished.

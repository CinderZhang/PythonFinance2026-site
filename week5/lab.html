Step 0 — Load what Week 4 left you

Load your weights and your Week 3 panel with the French columns. Build the portfolio's monthly return series, weights fixed and reset each month. Print the checks from prework Part A. Then subtract the risk-free column to get the excess series and print its annual mean, volatility and Sharpe ratio, labelled in-sample. The shape, not your answer:

weights sum 1.000   min 0.000   max 0.200 (cap 0.200)   months 120   gaps 0
portfolio  ann mean 19.45%  vol 14.02%  Sharpe 1.255   (in-sample, full window)

Step 1 — The label

Run two regressions on the excess series: on Mkt-RF alone, then on Mkt-RF, SMB and HML together. Report, for each: the alpha per year with its t-statistic, every beta with its t-statistic, and R². Then report the share of your portfolio's variance the market explains beside the average share for your single names from Lab 3 Step 4. The shape, not your answer:

                 alpha/yr   t     b_mkt   b_smb    b_hml    R²
market only      8.6%      3.8    0.76                     0.75
three factors    6.5%      3.3    0.83   −0.39   −0.00     0.82
market explains: portfolio 75%   average single name 30%  (range 12%..55%)

Compare with your prework prediction and say, in one sentence, where you were furthest off.

Step 2 — Read it like a committee

Write three sentences a committee could use, one per loading, in plain words with the number in it. The shape, not your answer:

Market 0.83: on average in this sample, a 10% market fall went with a fall of about 8 here.
Size −0.39: on average, other things equal, a year when small caps beat large by 10 points went with lagging the market by about 4.
Value 0.00: no tilt, consistent with the growth names and the staples offsetting.

Then the alpha. State it, state its t-statistic, and state what produced the weights it was measured on. If your weights came from an optimizer fitted on this window, the alpha is in-sample and you say so. If you want to claim any of it as skill, the burden is on you to say why.

Step 3 — Price your policy

Simulate your portfolio from the target weights over your window under three policies: the frequency you chose in prework, never rebalancing, and one other. For each report the number of resets, one-way turnover per year, drag at your cited cost, annual return, volatility, the largest drift of any weight from its target, and the biggest position at the end of the window. The shape, not your answer:

quarterly   resets 39   turnover 12.5%/yr   drag 1.2 bp/yr at 10 bp round trip   max drift 5.0 pp   end: MSFT 24.0%
never       resets  0   turnover  0         drag 0                                max drift 23.3 pp  end: MSFT 40.5%   (cap 20% on targets; holdings at twice it)

The cap binds your target weights; say how far holdings drifted above it between resets under each policy and, under "never", in which year they passed it. State whether you chose your frequency before or after seeing this table. Then write the sentence for the sheet: the policy, what it costs, and what it buys.

Step 4 — How bad did it get

Build the wealth path from your portfolio's monthly total returns and follow the four-line recipe in handout Part 4: wealth, running peak, drawdown, then the trough, the peak before it, and the months to recover. Report the worst month, the maximum drawdown with both dates and the recovery, and the count of months spent below a previous high. Do the same for your Week 3 benchmark. If a drawdown has not recovered by the end of your window, say so. The shape, not your answer:

portfolio  worst month 2020-02 −8.5%   max drawdown −21.3%  peak 2021-12 (wealth 3.887) → trough 2022-09 (3.061)  recovered 7 months later   55 of 120 months below a prior high
SPY        worst month 2020-03 −12.5%  max drawdown −23.9%  peak 2021-12 → trough 2022-09   recovered 15 months later

Check one number by hand: trough wealth divided by peak wealth, minus one, is your maximum drawdown. If it is not, the recipe is wrong somewhere.

Then two sentences: what the drawdown tells a holder that the annual volatility does not, and what your monthly series cannot see.

Step 5 — The hand-over sheet, and the alpha defence

Put it on one page, in the shape of handout Part 5: the portfolio, what it is, what it costs, how bad, the convention, what it cannot see. Every line carries a number and the sentence it supports. The in-sample warning is on the page.

The "what it cannot see" block has at least three entries, one sentence each: the window and its regime, how many years and what kind of decade; the universe, how the names were chosen and who is missing from it; and the fit, which numbers were computed on the window the weights were chosen on. Add anything specific to your panel, such as positions and liquidity not being modelled.

Then the defence. A committee member reads your alpha and says: your process adds value; the t-statistic is over two; put more capital behind it. Write the strongest version of their case, with the number that supports it. Then answer it or concede it. Conceding earns full marks. So does answering it well. Pretending the alpha is not on the page earns nothing.

Step 6 — What did you notice that I did not ask about?

The standing question.

Something this week behaved unexpectedly. A loading with the wrong sign for what you thought the portfolio was. Holdings that passed the cap under "never" sooner than you guessed. A drawdown that started in a year you would not have named, or one that has not recovered yet.

Write it down with evidence. This week it is not collected. Write it anyway; noticing with evidence is the habit the capstone memo runs on.

If you want to do this differently

You may depart from the method above. A defended departure earns full marks. State what you did instead, why, and what you gave up.

Three departures are welcome and worth a Step 6 finding: a threshold rebalancing policy with a band you choose and defend, which keeps holdings within the band; the same regressions on the CRSP monthly file with a longer history, which adds observations to every loading if the names existed then and their exposures held; or, if you want to cook rather than read the label, a simple long-only tilt toward one factor on your own panel, evaluated out of sample the Week 4 way, with its cost stated.

Two sources, minimum, and where you have professional access, one of them is the professional source. This week that is CRSP monthly returns and ff.factors_monthly on WRDS, for history.

Pending access is neither an excuse nor a penalty. Document the attempt, source, route tried, date, barrier, and why that source would have been independent, and use a second free source instead. The attempt counts; the outcome never does.

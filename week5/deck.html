What a committee actually asks

Not your Sharpe ratio.

  • What can go wrong?
  • How badly?
  • How often?
  • What will you do about it?
A risk report that overstates confidence is worse than none — it displaces the caution that would otherwise be there.

VaR, three ways

A 1-day 99% VaR of −3% says:

  • on 99% of days, the loss should be no worse than 3%
  • on 1% of days it will be worse — and VaR says nothing about how much
MethodAssumes
Historicalthe future resembles the sampled past
Parametricreturns are normally distributed
Monte Carloyour assumed process is right

All three assume something false. Which failure can you live with — and did you measure it?

Backtest it

var99  = returns.rolling(250).quantile(0.01).shift(1)
breach = returns < var99

That .shift(1) is Week 2 returning. Without it: look-ahead bias in a risk-management hat.

You measured model risk this morning: 1.77x.

Its silences are not reassurance.

The report a committee would accept

  1. The number — VaR and CVaR, with horizon, confidence, method
  2. The backtest — how often this method was wrong
  3. The concentration — where risk actually sits
  4. The leverage — gross and net
  5. What would have to happen to lose a stated amount
  6. The limitations — named specifically
  7. The action — decided in advance

6 is your Week 2 catalog. 7 is the one juniors omit and committees care most about.

A risk report is judged by what it admits, not what it claims.

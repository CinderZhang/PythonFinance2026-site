Part A — Reproduce the backtest

Run the rolling VaR backtest from handout.md Part 2 on any liquid series.

Then check one thing deliberately: remove the shift and run it again.

Record both breach rates. The version without the shift will look much better calibrated. Write one sentence explaining why that improvement is worthless.

This is the most important five minutes of prework in the course. A risk model that looks well calibrated because it can see the future is the exact failure this week exists to prevent.

Part B — Choose your rebalancing policy

Decide now, before you price anything: calendar, threshold, or never.

Write one sentence on why. If you choose threshold, state the band and why that band.

You will be asked in Lab 5 Step 4 whether you chose before or after seeing the cost. Choosing after is allowed and must be disclosed — but deciding now gives you a cleaner answer and a better defence.

Part C — Find a real transaction cost number

Find an actual estimate of round-trip trading cost for the kind of security in your portfolio. Not a number you assumed — one you can cite.

Places to look: broker commission schedules, published bid-ask spread statistics, academic estimates of market impact, fund annual reports that disclose trading costs.

Write down the number and where it came from. If you cannot find one, write down where you looked and what you will assume instead — an assumption you have labelled as an assumption is fine; an assumption presented as a fact is not.

Part D — Write one paragraph

Your portfolio breaches its risk limit tomorrow. Before you know anything about why, what would you do? Now: what would have to be true for that response to make things worse?

Write both halves. The second is the harder one and it is the one Lab 5 Step 6 grades.

Bring to the session: both backtest results with your explanation, your rebalancing policy, your cited cost number, and this paragraph.

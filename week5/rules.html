Week 5 — Rules and Conventions

Register: RULES. No teaching here — see handout.md.

No risk number without its backtest

Every VaR or CVaR figure you report carries: horizon, confidence level, method, and the historical breach rate of that method on comparable data.

A risk number without its failure rate is not a risk number. It is a decoration, and a dangerous one, because it displaces caution that would otherwise be there.

The shift rule

Any rolling risk estimate uses only data available strictly before the period it governs. In practice that means shifting the estimate forward by one period, and its absence is the most common defect in student risk code.

A VaR that looks well calibrated is more likely to be look-ahead contaminated than correct. Suspect it first.

Leverage is reported, always

Gross and net, on every portfolio, in every report. Inherited from Week 4; it does not lapse.

Policies are chosen before they are priced

Your rebalancing policy goes in writing during prework, before you compute its cost. You will be asked whether you chose before or after, and the honest answer is required.

Choosing after is legitimate if disclosed. Presenting a post-hoc policy as a prior principle is not.

Actions are pre-committed, and their failure scenario is named

A risk report that identifies a risk without proposing an action is incomplete.

An action without a named scenario in which following it is wrong has not been thought through. Every pre-committed response has one.

Limitations are specific

"This analysis has limitations" is not a limitations section. Each entry names what the report cannot see, why, and what the reader should therefore not conclude.

Model risk is included explicitly, evidenced by your own backtest ratio.

AI use

AI writes VaR code fluently and will omit the shift unless asked. It will not backtest unless told, will not report clustering, and will not name a single limitation.

It is also unusually confident in tone about risk numbers, which is exactly wrong for this artefact.

What must be yours: the method choice, the backtest and its interpretation, the rebalancing policy, the limitations, and the pre-committed action. Those are the report.

You must be able to explain every line of code you submit.

Reproducibility and citation

Unchanged: fresh-kernel runs, every number traceable to code, every source in source_log.md.

Cost assumptions are sourced. A round-trip cost you assumed without a source is stated as an assumption, not presented as a fact.

Week 7 — Prework

Register: TEACH. The bridge from six weeks of artefacts to one deliverable.

You arrive with: everything you built. This week assembles it.

Honest time budget: about 2 hours. More than usual, deliberately — the assembly is the work, and doing it before the session makes the session useful.

Part A — Gather

One directory. Everything from Weeks 1 through 6:

  • security_master.csv and source_log.md
  • your panel and data_quality_issues.md
  • your conventions statement and metrics
  • your portfolio weights and constraints
  • your risk report and limitations section
  • your valuation and source ledger
  • every notebook

If something is missing, find out now. Week 7 assembles; it does not rebuild. A missing artefact is a missing section of your deliverable.

Part B — Run everything, fresh kernel, in order

This will take longer than you expect and things will break. That is the point of doing it now rather than the night before the deadline.

Record for each failure: what broke, why, and whether you can fix it.

Common causes, in rough order of frequency:

  • a file path that only existed on the day you wrote it
  • a variable defined in a cell you later deleted
  • a data pull that returns something different now than it did then
  • a library behaviour that changed

The third one is worth pausing on. If a data source returns different values now than it did in Week 2, that is a finding, and it belongs in your deliverable — you have direct evidence of the vintage problem you were taught, in your own work.

Part C — Identify your load-bearing assumption

Across your whole analysis, which single assumption, if wrong, most changes your conclusion?

Write it down along with:

  • why you made it
  • what evidence supports it
  • how strong that evidence actually is
  • what would have to be true for it to be wrong

You will defend this one on video with a data point you have not seen. Choosing it carefully now is worth more than rehearsing later.

Choose the real one. There is a temptation to nominate an assumption you feel comfortable defending. The grader will be able to tell whether it is actually load-bearing, and defending a minor assumption well scores worse than defending the real one imperfectly.

Part D — Draft the finding paragraph

Write the opening paragraph of your deliverable: your conclusion, its range, and your confidence in it. One paragraph.

Bring it. One of these will be read cold by the room in the session, and every question the room asks is a defect you get to fix before submission.

Bring to the session: everything gathered, your fresh-kernel failure list, your load-bearing assumption with its evidence, and the finding paragraph.

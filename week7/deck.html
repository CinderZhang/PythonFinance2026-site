FIN 69000-003 · Financial Analytics with Python (with AI) · **defences**

Today

You present, or you are a discussant. Everyone is doing one of the two.

Tuesday Thursday
Cohort A present discussant
Cohort B discussant present; course evaluation

Order is posted. Be ready two slots early.

The clock

4 minutes. Then 2 minutes of questions.

You will be stopped at four minutes, mid-sentence if necessary.

That is not rudeness. There are thirteen people after you, and the clock is the only thing protecting the last one.

The four minutes
1 The pair and the decision — which gets the capital, and the condition 20s
2 The exhibit — both ranges, both dated prices, the gap as a % 60s
3 The flip point — the assumption, the value, inside or outside history 60s
4 Two triggers — observable · threshold · review date 30s
5 One check that caught something 30s

One slide: the exhibit. Notebook open for the questions.

Lead with the decision

Weak — three quarters of the slot, nothing decided:

"So I looked at two companies in auto retail. I pulled their filings, built a five-year pro-forma for each, and the models gave ranges of $240–330 and $95–140. Based on that I think the first looks more attractive…"

Strong — 17 seconds:

"We recommend A, and we hold that as long as its gross margin stays above roughly 16%. A trades at a 31% discount to our midpoint; B trades at 9%. Here is the exhibit."

If your first sentence contains "so", you are still narrating.

Banned

  • Company background
  • A tour of your methodology
  • "I pulled the data from EDGAR"
  • A slide of your code
  • An apology for what you did not finish

Every one of these is taking seconds from the flip point.

The two questions

They are about cells in your model, not about valuation.

"Show me the line that produces your flip point." "Your terminal value is most of your number. Which driver sits underneath it, and where did it come from?" "Your two discount rates differ. Say why, and confirm nothing else does."

"I would have to look" is an answer. Not finding it after looking is what this detects.

Your discussant's written question is asked too. You have seen the form, not the question.

Scored before the feedback

The score is written at the moment the defence happens.

Feedback comes after, in writing, within twelve hours, against this same five-part structure.

It names defects. It does not prescribe fixes.

Keeping those apart is what makes the help safe to accept — and what keeps the work yours.

Discussants

A good question names one of their numbers and finds a tension with another.
Weak "How did you pick your discount rate?"
Weak "Did you consider a recession?"
Strong "Your flip point is a 15.9% margin, but your low case already runs 16.2%. Why doesn't the low case already flip it?"
Strong "Both companies use a 10% cost of equity, but one carries twice the leverage. Is the comparison fair?"

Then listen, and write two lines: what you asked, what they said, whether it moved you.

Graded on your question and your record. Never on their performance.

Being wrong, well

"That changes my answer, and here is how much."

Graded as strength:

  1. An Edition A prediction the model demolished, named plainly
  2. A question you cannot answer, answered in the memo afterwards
  3. A recommendation you decline to make, with what evidence would separate them

Graded as nothing: pretending the sensitivity is small when your own table says otherwise; a range so wide it cannot be wrong.

After both days — finish

The three capstone objects — model, IC memo, video — are submitted after the defences, so the room can reach the work.

The memo ends with two sections most memos do not have:

  • What the defence exposed — named, with the number before and after; and what you were asked to change and did not, with your reason.
  • Edition A against the model — which predictions held, which did not, what the model showed instead.

Being right about everything in a page written before the model existed is the least believable outcome in this course.

Dates are on the assignment pages.

After this course

Keep the package. Update it when something in it stops being true.

The numbers age within a quarter. The habits do not:

Source every input. Date every figure. Check the year that already happened before projecting the ones that have not. Say what would change your mind, and at what value. Be able to find any number you have written.

That is the whole thing.

What "reproducible" actually means

Not "the code runs."

A colleague who has never seen your work can:

  • Understand the question, from the document alone
  • Find every input, with source and as-of date
  • Rerun it and get your numbers
  • See what you chose, and why
  • Find what it cannot support, without asking

The last two are the ones everyone skips.

The cold-read test

Give it to someone with no context. Watch them read.

Their first three questions are almost always:

  1. What was the actual question here?
  2. Where did this number come from?
  3. Why this way and not the obvious other way?
Every question they ask is a defect in the document, not in the reader.

Working block — the fresh-kernel audit

Run everything. Week 1 to Week 6. Clean kernel. In order.

Something will break. That is expected.

For each: what broke, why, and did you fix it or document it?

A documented break is honest. A silent one is not.

What the deliverable says first

Analysts bury the answer. Reports that get read do not.

SectionDoes what
The findingconclusion, range, confidence
What it rests onthe 2–3 assumptions actually driving it
What would change itsensitivity, as statements about the world
How it was builtdata, method, choices
What it cannot supportlimitations, specifically
Reproduce itsource ledger, how to run

Limitations come before the appendix. Burying them is a tell, and readers know it.

The defense

Your most load-bearing assumption. A data point you have not seen. On video. Unaided.

  1. State the assumption, and why
  2. What evidence supports it — how strong?
  3. Engage with the new data point
  4. Does it change the conclusion? By how much?
  5. If it does — say so

Bad defenses: restate more confidently · dismiss the data point · retreat into method.

All three are recognisable in ninety seconds.

"That changes my answer, and here is how" is the strongest sentence available to you.

Working block — rehearse in pairs

Defend your assumption to your partner.

Partners: your job is to push, not to be kind.

The question to ask each other:

What evidence would change your mind?

If you cannot answer that, you have found something — and it is better found here than on the recording.

Live defense round

Two or three of you. In front of the room. With a fresh data point.

The rest of us are watching for one thing:

the moment of update.

Limitations are an asset

Anyone can produce a number.

Only someone who understands the analysis can say precisely where it fails.

The limitations section is the only part of your work that proves you understood it.

Interviewers ask what is wrong with your model. "Nothing really" ends the conversation badly.

You have six weeks of specific, evidenced limitations. Almost nobody at your stage does.

Never write "this analysis has limitations." Write the limitation.

The strongest argument against yourself

What is the strongest argument that your conclusion is wrong?

Not the easiest. The strongest.

Then answer it — or concede it.

If writing it changes your mind, say so in the deliverable. That is graded as a success.

What did you notice that I did not ask about?

For the last time — and this time at term scale.

What you can now say you can do

Two sources minimum — and one of them professional where you have access. Pending access is documented, never penalised. The attempt counts; the outcome does not.
  • Reconcile two sources; tell a defect from a convention
  • Key work on a permanent identifier, and say what breaks without one
  • Detect contamination that produces clean-looking output
  • Run a catalog that constrains what you will claim
  • Compute metrics, state their conventions, verify them independently
  • Build a portfolio and demonstrate where its optimizer breaks
  • Produce a risk report that states its own measured failure rate
  • Value a company with every input dated, reported as a range
  • Say precisely what your work cannot support

That last one is what gets you hired. It is the one your competition cannot do.

Before you submit — do these

  1. Run everything on a fresh kernel, in order. Document what breaks.
  2. Lead the deliverable with the finding.
  3. Write the consolidated limitations — each with evidence and a non-conclusion.
  4. Get a cold read. Fix every question they asked.
  5. Assemble the package: code, source ledger, catalog, README, conventions.
  6. Record the defense, unaided.
  7. Write the strongest argument against your own conclusion.

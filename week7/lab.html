MSF Financial Modeling

Lab 7 — The Defence

Register: DO. Teaching is in handout.md; conventions in rules.md.

You arrive with: two companies valued to the same standard, one exhibit, a flip point with a number on it, two triggers, an Edition A page, and four minutes rehearsed out loud.

Where this goes next: out of this course. The last thing you do is submit the memo, the model and the video, revised with what the room told you.

Both sessions are defences, and you attend both. Read your row.

Tuesday Thursday
Cohort A you present you are a discussant
Cohort B you are a discussant you present; course evaluation

Cohorts are assigned and posted before the week. You are graded on your presenting day (the live defence) and on your discussant day (inside the checkpoint category). Missing your presenting day needs the make-up route in rules.md, arranged before the day, not after.

Step 1 — The defence, four minutes

One slide. The exhibit. Stand up.
1 The pair and the decision. Which company gets the capital, and the condition under which that holds. 20 sec
2 The exhibit. Both ranges against both dated prices; the gap for each, as a percentage. 60 sec
3 The flip point. The assumption that reverses the ranking, the value at which it reverses, and whether that value sits inside the company's own history. 60 sec
4 Two triggers. Observable, threshold, review date. One per company. 30 sec
5 One check that caught something. What you believed, what the filing said, what you changed. 30 sec

You will be stopped at four minutes, mid-sentence if necessary. That is not rudeness; it is the room's time, and there are thirteen people after you.

Lead with the decision. A committee that hears nothing after your first sentence must still know which company you chose.

Step 2 — Two questions, on your own numbers

Immediately after, two minutes. You will be asked two questions, and they will be about cells in your own model, not about valuation in general. Expect the shape:

  • "Show me the line that produces your flip point."
  • "Your terminal value is most of your number. Which driver sits underneath it, and where did that driver come from?"
  • "Your two discount rates differ. Say why, and confirm nothing else differs."

Have the notebook open and know where things are. "I would have to look" is an answer, and it is a better answer than a confident wrong one — but not being able to find your own number after looking is the thing this is designed to detect.

Your assigned discussant's written question is asked too. You have seen the question form, not the question.

Your score is written before any feedback is given. The defence is an assessment; what comes after it is help. Keeping those apart is what makes the help safe to accept.

Step 3 — Your discussant day

On the day you are not presenting, you are the assigned discussant for one classmate in the other cohort.

Before the session: read their exhibit and submit one written question that names one of their numbers and finds a tension with another. The prework has the standard.

In the session: ask it when called, listen to the answer, and write two lines afterwards — what you asked, what they said, and whether it changed your view of their recommendation.

Those two lines are what your discussant day collects. You are graded on the quality of your question and your record of the answer, never on their performance.

Step 4 — Take the feedback in writing

Within twelve hours of your presenting day you will have written comments in Brightspace, against the same five-part structure everyone was scored on. They name defects; they do not prescribe fixes. The fix is yours, which is what keeps the work yours.

Step 5 — Revise, and say what changed

Then finish. The memo, the model and the video are submitted after both defence days, so the room's questions can reach them.

Add one short section at the end of the memo, and keep it honest:

  • what the defence exposed, named specifically, not "helpful feedback";
  • what you changed, with the number before and after;
  • what you were asked and did not change, and why you still hold it.

"A question was asked that I could not answer, and here is the answer now" is a strong close. So is "I was asked to change this and I am not going to, for this reason." What earns nothing is a memo that reads as though the defence never happened.

Step 6 — Edition A, and what the model did to it

The last paragraph of the memo. Put your Edition A page beside the finished model:

  • which of your predictions held;
  • which did not, and what the model showed instead;
  • whether you were wrong about the ranking, the widths, or the assumption that mattered.

Being wrong here is normal and is graded as honesty, not error. Being right about everything in a page written before the model existed is the least believable outcome in this course, and it will be read closely.

Step 7 — What did you notice that I did not ask about?

The standing question, for the last time, at term scale.

Across seven weeks something recurred that no single lab asked about. A pattern in how data sources fail. A habit you built. A question this course raised and never answered. Something you now think is wrong with how any of this is normally done.

Write it down with evidence. This is the last and least constrained thing you will write here. Use it.

If you want to do this differently

The defence structure is fixed, because fourteen people share the clock.

The deliverable is not. A defended departure earns full marks: a different exhibit, a different way of expressing the gap, multiples carrying more weight than the discounted cash flow, stated and justified. If your honest conclusion is that neither company can be responsibly valued with what you could reach, that is a legitimate deliverable, provided you show what you built, what you found, and precisely why it does not support a ranking. Establishing that a number should not be produced is real analyst work.

Submit

  • In the room, your presenting day: the four-minute defence and the two answers. No upload.
  • In the room, your discussant day: your question and your two-line record.
  • After both days: the capstone model, the investment-committee memo, and the presentation video, on their Brightspace pages, with Step 5's revision section and Step 6's Edition A comparison inside the memo. Dates are on those pages.

After this course

Keep the package. Update it when something in it stops being true.

The specific numbers will age within a quarter. The habits will not: source every input, date every figure, check the year that already happened before projecting the ones that have not, say what would change your mind and at what value, and be able to find any number you have written.

That is the whole thing.
